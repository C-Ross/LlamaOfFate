# Character and Aspect Language

LlamaOfFate uses Fate Core aspects to describe notable truths about characters and scenes that can influence play.

## Characters

**Character**:
A person or creature represented in play.

## Aspects

**Aspect**:
A phrase that states a notable truth about its subject and can affect the story through an invoke or a compel.

**Character Aspect**:
An aspect describing a character's identity, circumstances, relationships, or personal complications. LlamaOfFate uses a flexible set of character aspects rather than a fixed five-aspect allotment.

**High Concept**:
A character's central identity or premise.

**Trouble**:
A recurring problem or complication in a character's life.

**Other Aspect**:
A character aspect that is neither the High Concept nor the Trouble.

**Situation Aspect**:
An aspect attached to a scene that describes a notable feature or circumstance in it.

**Consequence**:
An injury or other lasting complication a character accepts to absorb the shifts of a hit, expressed as an aspect.

**Invoke**:
Use an aspect's relevance to affect a roll or opposition, usually by spending a Fate Point or using a free invoke.

**Compel**:
Use an aspect to bring a complication into the story; the affected character can accept it for a Fate Point or pay a Fate Point to avoid it.

**Boost**:
A fleeting aspect that provides a free invoke and disappears once that invoke is used or the opportunity passes.

**Free Invoke**:
An invoke on an aspect that does not require spending a Fate Point, usually earned by creating an advantage or inflicting a consequence.

## Action Resolution

**Skill**:
A character's capability in a particular area, used to resolve actions.

**Ladder**:
The ordered scale of adjectives used to describe a skill rating, difficulty, or result.

**Roll**:
The random part of an action's result, combined with a skill rating and any applicable modifiers.

**Fate Die**:
A die whose faces contribute a result of minus one, zero, or plus one to a roll.

**Opposition**:
The difficulty an action must meet or exceed, provided either by a fixed challenge or by another character's roll.

**Outcome**:
The result of comparing an action's result with its opposition: failure, tie, success, or success with style.

**Shift**:
The numerical difference between an action's result and its opposition; positive shifts measure how decisively it succeeds.

**Overcome**:
An action to get past an obstacle or achieve a goal.

**Create an Advantage**:
An action to introduce a new aspect or make use of an existing one, potentially gaining free invokes.

**Attack**:
An action intended to harm or take an opponent out.

**Defend**:
An action intended to prevent an attack or an attempt to create an advantage from succeeding.

## Character Resources

**Fate Point**:
A resource spent to invoke aspects or refuse a compel, and gained through compels and other rules.

**Refresh**:
The minimum number of Fate Points a character begins a new scenario with.

**Stress**:
Short-lived harm a character absorbs during a conflict without taking a lasting consequence.

**Stress Track**:
A set of boxes a character can mark to absorb stress from a hit.

**Stunt**:
A special capability that gives a character a distinctive benefit in a defined circumstance.

**Consequence Severity**:
The degree of a consequence—mild, moderate, severe, or extreme—which determines how much of a hit it absorbs and how long it lasts.

**Consequence Recovery**:
The process of addressing a consequence so it can eventually be removed from a character.

## Structured Play

**Scenario**:
A connected story arc made up of scenes, after which characters' Fate Point totals are brought back in line with their Refresh.

**Conflict**:
A structured confrontation in which characters try to harm or take one another out; it can be physical or mental.

**Exchange**:
A cycle in a conflict in which each active participant gets an opportunity to act.

**Initiative**:
The order in which participants act during a conflict.

**Full Defense**:
A choice to give up one's action for an exchange in order to gain a bonus to defense rolls during that exchange.

**Concession**:
A character's voluntary withdrawal from a conflict before a roll, preserving some control over the outcome.

**Taken Out**:
The state of losing a conflict without conceding, leaving the victor to determine what happens to the defeated character.

**Challenge**:
A complex situation resolved through several overcome actions, often using different skills.

**Task**:
One obstacle or goal within a challenge, resolved as an overcome action.

**Contest**:
A structured competition between opposing sides, resolved through a series of rolls.

**Zone**:
An area within a conflict that helps describe where participants are and which actions or movement can reach them.

## Character Roles

**Player Character**:
A character controlled by a player and serving as one of the story's protagonists.

**Non-Player Character**:
A character controlled by the game master rather than a player.

**Main NPC**:
A significant non-player character with capabilities comparable to a player character.

**Supporting NPC**:
A non-player character with a simplified role who can take mild and moderate consequences.

**Nameless NPC**:
A minor non-player character represented with minimal detail and limited ability to withstand opposition.
